# COMMUNITY DISASTER WATCH — RESPONDER REPORT VERIFICATION

Implement a **fully working disaster report verification and response workflow** in the existing Community Disaster Watch (Nepal Operational Workspace) project.

Keep the implementation efficient. Use short, focused changes. Avoid unnecessary refactoring, repeated file inspections, and excessive AI usage.

## 1. Inspect Before Editing

- Inspect the existing project structure, database schema, APIs, authentication, dashboards, and report workflow.
- Identify existing report statuses, roles, assignment logic, and notification systems.
- Continue from the current code. Do not rebuild working features.

## 2. Implement the Complete Workflow

Citizen submits a report  
↓  
Report status: **Reported**  
↓  
Administrator reviews and performs initial triage  
↓  
Administrator assigns a responder or response team  
↓  
Responder investigates and submits a verification recommendation  
↓  
Administrator reviews the recommendation  
↓  
Administrator decides: **Verified**, **Rejected**, or **Needs More Information**  
↓  
If verified, administrator authorizes dispatch  
↓  
Responder acknowledges the assignment and records response progress  
↓  
Administrator reviews the outcome and closes the report as **Resolved**

Support reopening a resolved report when justified, with a recorded reason and audit history.

## 3. Responder Dashboard

Build a functional **Assigned Reports / Field Verification** section.

Responders must be able to:

- View reports assigned to them or their authorized teams.
- See incident type, description, location, time, submitted images, and priority.
- View report details and incident locations on the existing map.
- Accept or acknowledge an assignment.
- Record field observations and investigation notes.
- Add evidence photos when supported by the existing upload system.
- Submit one of these recommendations:
  - **Recommend Verified**
  - **Recommend Rejected**
  - **Request More Information**
  - **Unable to Verify**
- Explain the reason for the recommendation.
- Record visit time, verification time, and relevant incident conditions.
- Update response progress after dispatch.
- View the history and current status of their assigned reports.

Responders must not officially approve, reject, or close reports unless the existing authorization policy explicitly grants that permission.

## 4. Administrator Controls

Create or improve the administrator's report review screen.

Administrators must be able to:

- Review new reports and perform initial triage.
- Assign or reassign reports to eligible responders or teams.
- Set priority and record assignment instructions.
- View responder observations, evidence, and recommendations.
- Approve verification, reject the report, or request more information.
- Require a reason for rejection and important status changes.
- Authorize dispatch only after the report has been verified.
- Track acknowledgement, dispatch, progress, and resolution.
- Close reports as Resolved after reviewing the outcome.
- Reopen reports with a mandatory reason when necessary.

Show clear status badges, assignment details, timestamps, and an activity timeline.

## 5. Enforce Permissions and Status Rules

Implement authorization on the **backend**, not only in the UI.

- Citizens can submit reports and view their own reports according to existing privacy rules.
- Responders can access only reports assigned to them or their authorized teams.
- Administrators can review reports within their authorized jurisdiction.
- Superadmins retain existing system-level permissions.
- Responders cannot approve their own verification recommendations.
- The administrator's final decision must be separate from the responder's recommendation.
- Prevent unauthorized status changes, duplicate assignments, and invalid transitions.
- Validate every request on the server.
- Keep private reports and pending reports hidden from public maps and public feeds.
- Only reports approved for public display under existing policy may appear publicly.

Use transactions or equivalent safeguards where needed to prevent conflicting status updates.

## 6. Database, Evidence, and Audit History

Reuse the existing database design where possible. Add only the necessary fields, tables, and migrations.

Persist:

- Assignment and reassignment history.
- Responder observations and verification recommendations.
- Evidence references and upload metadata.
- Administrator decisions and reasons.
- Dispatch acknowledgement and response progress.
- Status changes, responsible user, timestamps, and resolution details.

Keep an append-only audit history for important decisions. Do not silently overwrite previous recommendations or decisions.

Use safe migrations. Preserve existing reports, users, assignments, and historical data.

Notify the appropriate users when:

Make all screens responsive and accessible. Include loading states, empty states, validation messages, error handling, and confirmation dialogs for important actions.
